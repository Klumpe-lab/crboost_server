## Reading the agreement rows: architecture, not noise

The B200 1g.23gb slice reproduces the full B200 bitwise in every stage, and the A100 3g.20gb slice reproduces the full
A100 bitwise: the same architecture gives the same bits, slice or card. Across architectures (sm_100 against sm_80 and
sm_75) every motion frame differs at floating-point level (trajectory differences under 0.01 px at the median) and a
few frames differ materially. Where, by tilt angle:

| Card | Tilt series | Frames that differ materially (angle: Δdefocus µm, Δmotion X / Y px) |
|---|---|---|
| A100 (and A100 3g, identical) | Position_13 (41 frames) | −70°: 0.19, 6.6 / 65.0 · −67°: 0.002, 15.6 / 5.9 · −64°: 0.03, 2.6 / 5.8 · +35°: 0, 1.3 / 3.3 · +41°: 0.007, 1.5 / 2.1 |
| A100 | Position_1 (27 frames) | −25°: 0, 4.3 / 1.4 |
| RTX 6000 | Position_13 | −70°: 0.02, 7.7 / 77.9 · −67°: 0.04, 2.9 / 26.2 · −64°: 0.08, 0.6 / 3.0 · +41°: 0.07, 0.9 / 2.0 · +50°: 0.009, 4.1 / 1.3 |
| RTX 6000 | Position_1 | −46°: defocus 1.58 against the pilot's 7.47 · −37°: 1.59 against 6.27 · −25°: 0.008, 5.1 / 1.5 |

The material differences sit on the highest tilts (−70° to −61°, +35° to +50°) of Position_13, where there is least
signal, and on Position_1, the pilot's poorest series (26 tilts kept, −46° to +29°, defocus fits scattered between
1.6 and 4.8 µm along the series). On Position_1's −46° and −37° frames the pilot's B200 fits (7.5 and 6.3 µm) are the
outliers against their neighbours (1.6–2.0 µm); the RTX 6000's 1.6 µm agree with the neighbours. So the architecture
does not make one card right and another wrong: a fit with no signal lands where rounding pushes it.

Alignment (AreTomo2, staged from the pilot's own averages): Position_13, 13_2 and 13_3 identical to the pilot's on
every card, every tilt (0 px, 0°). Position_1: on the A100 7 of 26 tilts shift by more than 1 px (up to 208 px), on
the RTX 6000 11 of 26 (up to 704 px): a different solution on an ill-conditioned series. Tilt-series CTF (defocus),
Warp reconstruct (tomogram centre, correlation 1.000) and pytom (score-map centre equal to 3e-8 on every card) are
architecture-stable.

Consequences (roadmap 47): one architecture per project, and a failed item re-run on the same architecture; the Warp
stages stay on the B200 pool (full cards and slices, 256 devices), which already starts at once; the RTX 6000 and A100
nodes carry CPU work (a GPU is the ticket onto `g`) and could carry pytom. A whole-chain pilot on A100s compared with
the B200 pilot (`compare_projects.py`) would say whether the frame-level differences reach the tomograms and picks of
good tilt series; about 135 billing-h. A report to Warp's author needs a frame with signal where architectures
disagree materially, or a systematic bias of one architecture; neither is in this sample.
